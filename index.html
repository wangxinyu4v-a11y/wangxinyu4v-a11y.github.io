About

Xinyu Wang

I am an Assistant Professor at the Yukawa Institute for Theoretical Physics, Kyoto University. My research combines mechanics, topology, symmetry, computation, and soft matter to understand and design materials with programmable mechanical response.

Before joining YITP, I was a postdoctoral researcher in Physics at the University of Michigan and previously at HKUST. I received my Ph.D. in Civil Engineering from Southeast University and spent two years as a visiting Ph.D. researcher in Physics & Astronomy at the University of Pennsylvania.

Research

Current directions

Topological & nonlinear mechanics

I investigate topological Maxwell lattices and related near-isostatic systems, with a focus on stress localization, soft modes, states of self stress, nonlinear deformation, symmetry reduction, and fracture.

History-dependent mechanics

I am developing frameworks for how local topology and rigidity reorganize under irreversible deformation, and how interacting local soft and load-bearing states generate collective mechanics in heterogeneous systems.

Soft & programmable materials

My work in liquid crystals, liquid crystal elastomers, and soft matter explores how structure, anisotropy, and active or stimulus-driven processes can create programmable mechanical behavior.

Architected materials

I use geometry and mechanics to design structures with unusual deformation, deployment, instability, and damage response, supported by nonlinear finite element modeling and reduced-order mechanical models.

People & opportunities

Interested in working with me?

I welcome motivated students and researchers interested in theoretical and computational mechanics. Current projects are available in topological mechanics, nonlinear mechanics, soft materials, and architected structures.

Prior experience with finite element methods, topological mechanics, or symmetry methods is useful but not required. Necessary tools can be learned through the research itself, with projects scoped to match the researcher’s background.

Nonlinear topological mechanics

Symmetry, mode selection, phase transitions, and nonlinear response in Maxwell lattices.

Multi-domain TMMs

Interactions between neighboring soft and load-bearing states across interfaces and heterogeneous domains.

History-dependent mechanics

How deformation history changes local topology, rigidity, stress transmission, and damage pathways.

Soft topological systems

Connections between programmable soft materials, liquid crystal elastomers, and topological transport or mechanics.

Students and collaborators: If one of these directions overlaps with your interests, please get in touch with a short description of your background and what you would like to explore.
